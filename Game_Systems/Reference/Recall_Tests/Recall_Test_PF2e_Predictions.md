# Recall Test — Pathfinder 2e Predictions

**Written before consulting any source.** Companion to the d20 test; the point is comparative.

5e is the most-reproduced tabletop content that exists, so "structural recall is strong even on
obscure entries" may have measured training redundancy rather than recall. Pathfinder 2e is far
less represented, and its 2023 Remaster is a cleaner edition-hazard probe than the 5e 2014/2024
question because it changed names rather than numbers.

**Prediction before testing:** structural recall will be *noticeably worse* than on 5e — probably
right on the system's architecture (degrees of success, action economy, proficiency structure) and
materially wrong on specific creature and spell numbers. If that does not happen, the earlier
finding generalises further than expected.

Pathfinder 1e is not tested here; it is a separate question and a 3.5-derived system.

---

## System architecture — expected strong

**P1. Degrees of success** — confident
d20 + modifiers vs DC, four degrees. Critical success on beating the DC by 10 or more; success on
meeting or beating it; failure below it; critical failure 10 or more below. A natural 20 improves
the degree by one step, a natural 1 worsens it by one step — applied to the degree, not the total.

**P2. Action economy** — confident
Three actions per turn, plus one reaction, plus any number of free actions. Activities consume a
stated number of actions.

**P3. Proficiency ranks** — moderate, and the exact untrained handling is the uncertain part
Proficiency bonus is character level plus a rank modifier: Trained +2, Expert +4, Master +6,
Legendary +8. Untrained is +0 **and does not add level**. Low confidence on that last clause
specifically.

## Conditions — expected moderate

**P4. Frightened** — moderate
Frightened X imposes a −X status penalty to all checks and DCs. The value decreases by 1 at the
end of each of the affected creature's turns.

**P5. Clumsy** — moderate
Clumsy X imposes a −X status penalty to Dexterity-based checks and DCs, which includes AC, Reflex
saves, Dexterity-based skill checks, and attack rolls using Dexterity.

## Specific content — expected weak

**P6. Fireball** — low
3rd rank. Range 500 feet. 20-foot burst. 6d6 fire damage, basic Reflex save. Heightened: +2d6 per
rank above 3rd.

**P7. Owlbear** — low, chosen to mirror the 5e test directly
Creature 4. AC 21. HP around 70. Fortitude +13, Reflex +9, Will +9. Speed 25 feet.
Beak +14, 2d8+7 piercing. Claw +14, 1d10+7 slashing. Probably has a grab or knockdown rider and a
scent ability.

## Edition hazard — the Remaster

**P8. What the 2023 Remaster changed** — low to moderate
Driven by removing OGL-derived material. Alignment removed as a mechanic. Schools of magic removed
and replaced with a different categorisation. Spell "levels" renamed to "ranks". Some spells
renamed — believed *Magic Missile* became *Force Barrage*. The classic chromatic and metallic
dragons replaced with new Paizo-owned dragon types. Some creature names dropped for being Product
Identity.

**Confidence note:** this is the prediction most likely to be partly confabulated. Remaster
specifics are recent, less written about than 5e's 2024 revision, and consist of many small renames
— exactly the "secondary element" shape that the d20 test found to be the failure mode.
